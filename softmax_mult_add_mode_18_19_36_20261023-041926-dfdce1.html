Candidate examined

FPMult_PrepModule.$mul$softmax.v:2386$221(sum_in=FPMult_16), 16 instances — the only candidate. It is assign Mp = ({1'b1,a[9:0]} * {1'b1,b[9:0]}) at softmax.v:2386, an 11x19-free 11x11 unsigned product. The 16 instances come from the 16 expunits (softmax.v:1073-1080 in mode3_exp and softmax.v:1382-1389 in mode7_exp), each holding one FPMult (softmax.v:1999) -> FPMult_16 (softmax.v:2180) -> FPMult_PrepModule (softmax.v:2259).

It is left unconverted. The rewrite is implementable but raises the objective. Reasons, in order of weight:

  1. DSP tiles. The baseline already has all 16 of these products on DSP tiles, two to a tile in two_mult_18x19 mode, 8 tiles total (the design's whole DSP usage). A mult_add_int_18x19 block takes a tile alone, so converting k of the 16 gives k + ceil((16-k)/2) tiles = 8 + ceil(k/2). Every non-zero k costs tiles: converting all 16 gives 16 tiles, +8 tiles = +2,030,232 min-width transistor areas on a baseline used-logic area of 4.15428e+07 (+4.9%).
  2. What comes back is far smaller. The only soft logic the block can absorb is the Mp field of pipe_1 in FPMult_16 (softmax.v:2299), 22 flip-flops per instance, 352 in total. It cannot absorb the operand registers: pipe_0 (softmax.v:2285) is read bit-by-bit by everything else in FPMult_PrepModule — Sa/Sb (2376-2377), Ea/Eb (2378-2379), InputExc from ANaN/BNaN/AInf/BInf (2366-2372), and the pipe_0[25:16]/pipe_0[8:0] fields forwarded into pipe_1 — so pipe_0 stays in soft logic in full and the block's ax/ay registers are added on top of it, not instead of it. At one BLE per freed flip-flop and 27905 per cluster, 352 flip-flops are worth at most ~9.8e+05 area, and in practice less because those flip-flops pack into clusters that hold other logic. Net area change is at best about +1.05e+06 (+2.5%) and realistically +3 to +4%.
  3. No delay to win. The slowest stage, 9.44889 ns, and the next one, 9.371 ns, both sit in modules with no multiply on the path (a register through one LUT, a 3-cell carry chain, ten LUTs into a flip-flop). The candidate is not on either, so no conversion of it can shorten the clock period; the best possible outcome is leaving 9.44889 ns untouched. The converted stage itself would be fine (register -> wire 1.52 -> block setup 0.019 = ~1.54 ns in; 0.060 + 2.140 + 0.019 = 2.219 ns inside; out of result 0.060 + 0.96 into FPMult_ExecuteModule's mux/5-bit add/OR-reduce into pipe_2), i.e. no new stage near 9.37 ns, but also no gain.
  4. Wirelength does not pay for it either. Doubling DSP occupancy from 8 to 16 tiles spreads the 16 multipliers across the device and lengthens the 11-bit operand nets from a_reg/LUTout_reg2 (softmax.v:1999) and the 22-bit result nets; the one hop saved (DSP -> pipe_1 -> ExecuteModule becomes DSP -> ExecuteModule) does not offset it. Breaking even would need a ~2.5-4% wirelength drop, which is the wrong direction.

With delay flat and area up by 2.5-4%, area x delay x wirelength rises above the baseline 8.04768e+13 for every k from 1 to 16. Partial conversion has the same sign: each additional pair of conversions costs one tile (253,779) and returns at most 44 flip-flops (~1.2e+05).

Two further costs the rewrite would carry, recorded so the decision is reproducible

  • Reset value mismatch. On rst, the original clears pipe_0 to 0, and the implicit leading ones are concatenated after that register, so on the first cycle after a reset release pipe_1's Mp field is {1'b1,10'b0} * {1'b1,10'b0} = 2^20, not 0. The block's operand registers hold the post-concatenation value and clear to 0, so its result would be 0 on that cycle. Matching it needs a correction outside the block (two flip-flops delaying rst plus one LUT OR-ing bit 20 of result when the previous cycle was in reset and this one is not) — correct and cheap, but it adds logic rather than removing it, and it argues against the conversion rather than for it.
  • No reset in the design as wired. FPMult ties FPMult_16's rst to 1'b0 (softmax.v:2180), and FPMult_16's pipeline registers are free-running there. The block has no initial value and only reset clears it, so in the design as built no block register would ever be cleared, while the original's registers start at 0. The equivalence testbench drives rst into FPMult_16 directly and would not expose this, but the behaviour requirement would not actually hold in the design. There is no power-up-safe signal inside FPMult_16 to derive a reset from.
