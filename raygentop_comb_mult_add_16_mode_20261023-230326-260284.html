What the source says about the two candidate groups

matmult (raygentop.v:333-351, one instance at raygentop.v:177). Nine products are registered: am11 <= Ax*m11 … am33 <= Az*m33 (raygentop.v:335-343). The three sums (raygentop.v:348-350) are Cx <= am11[30:15] + am12[30:15] + am13[30:15], i.e. each registered product is truncated by itself (bits [30:15]) before the 16-bit sum, and the sum is registered into Cx/Cy/Cz. The amXX registers are read nowhere else; Ax/Ay/Az and m11…m33 come from outside the module.

bilinearintrp (raygentop.v:2860-2886, one instance at raygentop.v:2012). Nine products are registered: i1r <= ul*rul … i3b <= wl*bwl (raygentop.v:2874-2882), operands 8x7 into 15-bit registers (255*127 = 32385, so exact). The sums are combinational module outputs, assign r = (i1r+i2r+i3r) etc. (raygentop.v:2884-2886), each truncated to 7 bits. ul, vl, wl each feed three products (so they must stay outside any block, and do: they would be ax); i1r…i3b are read only by those three assigns.

So in both groups the shape is the one the candidate table states: three products, one register stage between the products and their sum.

Why every candidate is left

All nine bilinearintrp products (raygentop.v:2874$348 … 2882$356) — left, one reason: the sum cannot follow the products into the block without either a second block in series or an extra register stage, and the block costs more DSP tile area than the soft adders it would remove.

  • The three products of one colour are formed in one stage and summed in the next; a block is combinational, so folding one add into a block means either (a) two blocks in series in one stage — 1.314 (register to first block) + 2.14 + 1.17 (block to block) + 2.14 + 0.621 (last block to register) = 7.39 ns, or (b) a soft adder feeding bx — 1.314 + 1.032 (adder level) + 0.96 (wire into the block's input) + 2.14 + 0.621 = 6.07 ns. Both are far past the 4.84712 ns slowest stage, so either one becomes the new critical path.
  • Retiming instead (register the sum rather than the products: sr <= ul*rul + vl*rvl + wl*rwl, assign r = sr[6:0]) reproduces r,g,b on the same cycles, but then one stage holds three multiplies and two adds — at least 1.314 + 2.14 + 0.96 + 1.032 + 1.032 + 0.621 ≈ 7.1 ns with hard multiplies, worse still with blocks in series. The baseline is fast precisely because the multiply and the adds sit in different stages.
  • Packing two products into one 16x16 multiply does not work here. The three products of a colour share no operand, and the pairs that do share one (ul*rul, ul*gul, ul*bul) are 15 bits each, so two lanes need a 30-bit ay; packing (ul<<8|vl) * (rvl<<8|rul) puts the wanted lane ul*rul+vl*rvl at bit 8 contaminated by the carry out of vl*rul (up to 126), and removing that carry needs the product itself. The 7-bit output does not help: mod 128 of a lane does not remove an additive carry into it.
  • Area: these nine products sit three to a tile (three tiles). A comb_mult_add_16 takes a tile alone, so converting k of them adds k tiles and frees at most floor(k/3); all nine would be 9 tiles against 3, i.e. +6 x 253779 = +1.52e6 (+32% area) to remove three 7-bit three-input adds and at most nine 15-bit product registers (a few clusters at 27905 each). bilinearintrp is not on the critical path, so there is no delay to win back.

All nine matmult products (raygentop.v:335$15 … 343$23) — left, one reason: the block's add happens before any truncation and one cycle too early for this sum, and a multiply-only swap buys at most 1.67% delay with nothing else.

  • The design sums am11[30:15] + am12[30:15] + am13[30:15], which is not (am11+am12+am13)[30:15] (the dropped low 15 bits carry 0, 1 or 2), so the per-product truncation has to survive. The block can express it — with bx = (am12[30:15] + am13[30:15]) << 15, result[30:15] = p11[30:15] + am12t + am13t exactly — but am12/am13 hold the previous cycle's products, so Ax/m11 would need a new delay stage (32 added flip-flops), and the resulting stage is register -> 16-bit soft adder -> block -> register = 1.314 + 1.032 + 0.96 + 2.14 + 0.621 ≈ 6.07 ns (even counting the adder's own output wire only once, ≥ 5.1 ns), i.e. a new critical path well past 4.84712 ns, to remove one 16-bit adder and gain a tile.
  • Converting one product alone with bx tied to 0 is the only neutral-tile option: nine products pack two to a tile, so eight repack with no tile saved and no tile added, and the swap removes no soft logic at all (the adders stay where they are). Its whole upside is the 2.14 ns block against the ≈2.217 ns the baseline multiply contributes (4.84712 - 1.42 - 1.21), and it is capped by the second path at 4.766 ns: best case 4.766, i.e. -1.67% delay, with area unchanged and wirelength free to move either way. The seed-1 report names the critical multiply only by shape (all nine products have the same shape), so eight of the nine choices gain nothing at all. I do not expect that to lower area x delay x wirelength, and converting two or three products to cover the choice pushes the tile count to 6 for matmult against 5 (+253779, +5.3% area) for at most a few percent of delay — a net loss.

The objective is area x delay x wirelength (delay register to register, 2026-10-20): VPR's total used logic block area (I/Os and empty grid tiles excluded) times the critical path times the total routed wirelength. Each term is the median over the loop's seeds; the product is the product of the medians. Beside every product stand the ratios against the baseline, A/A0 (area), D/D0 (critical path), W/W0 (wirelength) and their product, so the normalization can be read off. After each measured rewrite, the planner is given every measurement so far and asked for a revision. A revision is built and checked like the first rewrite, and the lowest product is kept, a later rewrite having to beat it by more than 4.32%. The loop stops when the planner declines to revise, when a revision does not lower the best product by more than 4.32%, when a revision fails its checks, or when 9 revisions have been made.
