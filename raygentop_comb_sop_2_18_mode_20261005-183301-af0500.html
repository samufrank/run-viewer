Rewrite rules

A stage is the logic between one register and the next. A block is one instance of the target mode. These rules hold for any synchronous design on any FPGA. Numbers that depend on this architecture are in the last section; use them, do not restate them as rules.

Must hold

Going against these produces a wrong design, not a slower one.

Observability. Only a block's output is visible. A value read by anything other than the arithmetic being taken into the block (another expression, a module output port, another module) cannot go inside a block. Keep it outside, or recompute it for those readers. Check the source; detection does not see port consumers. Operands are exempt: the block takes a copy and the original signal still exists.

Register control. A register carries its reset, enable and initial value. These modes hold no registers, so taking a register inside deletes that behaviour. If anything depends on it, keep the register and drive it from the block's result, or leave the site alone.

Arithmetic. The block computes the mode's expression on signed ports. Match the original bit for bit or leave the site alone. Keep every term of the original expression, including ports every instantiation ties to a constant. A term that goes on no block port (an addend beside the products) stays outside the block, added to its result; the flow flattens the design and removes an add of a constant, so such a term puts nothing on the stage and rule 2 does not count it. A bound on a port's values may be used only if the plan states it and names its source in the design, on a line of its own beginning ASSUMES:; such a rewrite is recorded as verified under that assumption. Fold a term only when it is constant inside the module itself, and say so in the plan.

Weigh

The clock period is the delay of the slowest stage in the whole design. A stage's delay is the sum of everything on it: each block's fixed delay, each wire, each soft cell. Each relationship below names what it does to delay and to area. If a plan goes against one, say why.

1. Slowest stage → all outputs

Your task names the design's slowest stage and what is on it.

Slowest stage → delay

Relationship: A rewrite that does not touch the slowest stage cannot make the design faster, and must leave every stage no slower than it was. A rewrite that does touch it must make it shorter, not merely different. Compare every stage you change against it.

Slowest stage → area

Relationship: A rewrite that cannot shorten the clock period is paid for in tiles alone. Say so in the plan.

2. Block to register → delay

Relationship: A block's result goes to a register, or into the next block, with nothing but wire between. Any soft arithmetic between a block and its register is added to the stage.

3. Blocks in series → all outputs
Blocks in series → delay

Relationship: Each block in series adds its own delay plus one wire. Sum them with the wire in and the wire out, and compare against the slowest stage. That comparison, not a fixed count, says how many fit.

Blocks in series → area

Relationship: None. Series or side by side, the same blocks take the same tiles.

4. Arithmetic inside the block → all outputs

Arithmetic inside the block is what the mode's expression computes rather than what sits in series with it: a multiply and one addend, or two products and their sum, depending on the mode.

Arithmetic inside the block → delay

Relationship: Free. The block's delay is the mode's own and does not rise because its adder is used. This is where a conversion wins: soft logic leaves the stage and the block's delay already covers the work. An adder feeding the block's addend port is mapped into the block; an adder reading the block's result is on the path after it (rule 2). A block whose addend port is tied to a constant takes in nothing; if the multiply it replaces is already on a tile, the conversion changes the tile count and nothing else.

Arithmetic inside the block → area

Relationship: Flip-flops fall where product registers go. Do not count on LUT savings.

5. Registers → all outputs
Registers → delay

Relationship: A register may move only if no stage becomes longer and the module's latency is unchanged. If one must be added to keep latency, put it where it splits the longest stage. Every other operand entering that stage must be delayed by the same amount, or the stage adds values from different cycles.

Registers → area

Relationship: A register placed mid-chain costs operand-delay registers that a register after the chain does not.

6. Tiles → area

Relationship: A block occupies one DSP tile in one mode. Do not assume the multiply it replaces frees a tile: the synthesis tool may already have placed that multiply on a tile shared with others. Count the blocks against the tiles the unmodified design uses and the device's count, both in your task.

No arrangement of comb_sop_2_18_mode lowers the area-delay product of this design, so nothing is converted. Source evidence and the per-candidate accounting follow.

Group 1 — matmult products (raygentop.v:335$15 … raygentop.v:343$23, all nine). Left: the baseline truncates each product before the sum.
raygentop.v:335-343 registers each product full width (am11 <= Ax * m11, reg[31:0]), and raygentop.v:348-350 sums only a slice of each register: Cx <= am11[30:15] + am12[30:15] + am13[30:15] (likewise Cy, Cz). Each addend is an independently truncated product — low 15 bits dropped and bit 31 dropped. A fused ax*ay + bx*by adds at full precision and can only be truncated after the sum, which differs from the sum of truncated products by the carries out of the discarded low bits. That is exactly the disallowed row of the arithmetic table ("original truncates each product before the sum → no"). Additionally Ax/m11 are 16-bit operands of unstated signedness arriving from top-level ports (rgconfigmemory matrix coefficients, raygentop.v:371-388), so an exact 16-bit unsigned value has no representation on the 18/19-bit signed ports without a bound. Both reasons independently rule these out.

This matters beyond matmult: the design's slowest stage (4.845 ns) and second slowest (4.766 ns) are both matmult product multiplies feeding am* registers. Since matmult cannot be converted exactly, no legal rewrite touches the slowest stage, so by rule 1 the clock period stays at 4.84502 ns and any conversion is paid for in tiles alone — it can only raise the ADP.

Group 2 — bilinearintrp products (raygentop.v:2874$348 … raygentop.v:2882$356, all nine). Exactly representable, but every arrangement costs tiles with no delay gain.
These do form three clean shared reductions: r = i1r + i2r + i3r, g = …, b = … (raygentop.v:2884-2886), with i1r…i3b read nowhere else (grep confirms the only readers are those three assigns) and held in plain regs with no reset or enable, so observability and register-control would both be satisfiable. Operands ul/vl/wl are 8-bit unsigned and rul…bwl are 7-bit unsigned, all narrower than the 18/19-bit signed ports, so zero-extension is exact; the 7-bit output wrap at assign r = (…) is reproduced by the low bits of the wider result. The arithmetic is legal. The economics are not:

  • Full fusion, 3 products per channel. The mode takes only two product terms, so the third needs a second block in series with the first block's partial sum entering as bx = partial, by = 1. That is 2 blocks × 3 channels = 6 blocks, 6 tiles replacing the 3 tiles the nine 9×9 products share today (packer puts four 9-bit multiplies per tile; a comb_sop_2_18 block takes its tile alone, rule 6) → 12 tiles total. The fused stage is clk-to-Q 0.06 + reg→block 2.25 + block 2.14 + block→block 1.17 + block 2.14 + block→reg 2.25 = 10.01 ns, more than double the 4.845 ns critical path. Ruled out by rule 3 (blocks in series) and rule 1.
  • Partial fusion, one block per channel (two products), third product left on a tile and added outside. 3 blocks = 3 tiles alone, plus 1 tile for the three remaining 9×9 products = 4 tiles where 3 sufficed → 7 tiles total. Latency is preservable (register the block output and keep one soft add level before r), but the new stage is clk-to-Q 0.06 + 2.25 + 2.14 + 2.25 = 6.7 ns at median wire, and even at the baseline's own measured wire figures (1.42 in, 1.21 out) it only matches the existing ~4.83 ns multiply-to-register stage — it never shortens it, because the critical path lives in matmult. Net effect: +1 tile (253 779 min-width transistor areas, ~5 % of used logic-block area), delay unchanged at best. ADP rises.

There is no tile-neutral or tile-saving arrangement: every block consumes a whole tile, while the baseline packs four of these 9-bit multiplies per tile, so fusing bilinearintrp always trades dense packing for exclusive tiles.

Conclusion. The only exactly-convertible candidates (Group 2) cannot touch the critical path and strictly increase tile count; the candidates on the critical path (Group 1) cannot be converted exactly. Every candidate rewrite leaves delay at 4.84502 ns and area above 4.7578e+06, i.e. an ADP above the 2.30516e+07 baseline. No introduced operations or datatypes; no files changed.
