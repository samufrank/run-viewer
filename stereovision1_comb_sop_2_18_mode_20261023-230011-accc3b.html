The "4 products in one tree" of the candidate rows is the top-level pairing at sv_chip1_hierarchy_no_mem lines 316-336, 368-378, 400-405, e.g. corr_out_10 <= {corr_out_1_p0[15],corr_out_1_p0[15],corr_out_1_p0} + {corr_out_1_n0[15],corr_out_1_n0[15],corr_out_1_n0};. The two 16-bit values added there are outputs of two different wrapper_corr_* instances; each has already passed through corr's own corr_out_tmp/corr_out registers and (for the normalized paths) the wrapper_norm* divider pipeline, and each is read by other logic. So the four products of a tree can never meet inside one block: only the two products of a single corr/corr_seq sum are fusable, which is exactly ax*ay + bx*by.

A correct T1/T2 rewrite exists but loses on the objective

Per instance it would be: one comb_sop_2_18 with ax = in_l_re, ay = in_r_re, bx = in_l_im, by = in_r_im (zero-extended; the design's operands are unsigned input[7:0], so the block's unsigned reading is right), one 16-bit register taking result[15:0] in place of the pair lrexrre_reg/limxrim_reg, and corr_out_tmp fed from that register. Latency is preserved (operands -> result is 2 register stages before and after: product register then sum register), and result[15:0] equals the original (l_re*r_re + l_im*r_im) truncated to 16 bits, since each product is exact in 16 bits and the original sum wraps at 16. It is left unconverted anyway, for cost:

  • Tile cost. Every multiply in the design is one of these candidates: 64x2 + 12x2 = 152 mult_9x9_fixed_pt, which is exactly the baseline's 35 tiles x 4 plus 12 more on the remaining 7, i.e. all 42 tiles. A comb_sop_2_18 takes a DSP tile alone, so each converted sum turns half a tile's worth of multiplies into a whole tile. Converting all 76 sums gives 76 blocks on 76 tiles: +34 tiles, +8.63e6 area (+34% of the 2.56155e7 baseline). Converting a subset of N gives 38 + N/2 tiles plus the leftovers' packing, always above 42. There is no subset with no tile growth.
  • Soft logic recovered is far smaller. Each conversion removes one 16-bit adder and one 16-bit product register (two product registers become one sum register); corr_out_tmp, corr_out and the new_data enable all stay. Generously 20 BLEs per instance, 76 x 20 = 1520 BLEs ~ 152 clusters ~ 4.2e6. Net area still +4.4e6 to +6.1e6, i.e. +17% to +24%.
  • No delay to win. The slowest stage (5.47292 ns) and the next (5.427 ns) are both corr product registers: wire 1.73 into a hard multiply, multiply ~2.29, wire 1.45 into the flip-flop. Every one of the 152 product stages has this shape, so the clock only moves if all 76 sums convert. The replacement stage is register -> wire -> block -> wire -> register: 1.732 + 2.14 + 1.314 = 5.19 ns at the median, 1.417 + 2.14 + 0.933 = 4.49 ns at the best sampled wires, 1.940 + 2.14 + 1.729 = 5.81 ns at the worst. The block is only 0.15 ns faster than the multiply it replaces; the win is wire-dominated and could as easily be a loss, and 76 blocks on 76 tiles spread further apart than 42 will lengthen exactly those wires and the routed wirelength (153334 baseline). To pay for +17% area the clock would have to fall below 4.65 ns, under the best-case block stage, with wirelength flat. It cannot.
  • No way to amortize a tile over more arithmetic. Checked: within one wrapper_corr_20/wrapper_corr_10, all 21 (resp. 11) corr instances share out_r1/out_r2 as in_r_re/in_r_im (lines 1247-1267, 1590-1610), so two instances' products could in principle be field-packed into one 18x19 multiply, ax = {in_l_re_k, in_l_re_j}, ay = out_r1. It does not work: each sub-sum is 17 bits wide and the widest usable shift inside ax[18] with 8-bit operands is 10, so the upper field's partial products corrupt bits 9..15 of the lower result. The 8-bit operands cannot be made to fill the block, so one block can carry at most these two products.
