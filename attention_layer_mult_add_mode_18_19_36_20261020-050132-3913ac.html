Each product moved into a block therefore costs about half a tile (about 126889 area units) more than it costs today, and the only soft logic a block could absorb for these candidates is the odd flip-flop (see per-candidate notes), worth on the order of one to two clusters (27905 each) per product. signedmul.$mul$attention_layer.v:741$74(sum_in=attention_layer)#1 and #2 — left. Grouped: one reason covers both. These are the 128 signedmul products (64 feeding the 64-product vecmat_add tree for qiki, 64 feeding the two 32-product vecmat_add_32 trees for softmulv_part1/2). The block's bx/chainin adder, which is the only thing that would pay for the dedicated tiles, cannot carry these sums:

  • signedmul (lines 722-756) multiplies magnitudes: a_new = |a|, b_new = |b|, result = a_ff*b_ff. What enters the adder tree is not that product but c = (b_sign_ff==a_sign_ff) ? result_ff[26:12] : (~result_ff[26:12]+1'b1) (line 738) — a bit slice of bits 26:12 (so bits 31:27 are dropped and the low 12 bits truncated), conditionally two's-complemented in 15 bits, then zero-extended to 16 bits, so c[15] is always 0. The qadd2 tree (line 765, c = a + b) then sums these 16-bit values modulo 2^16.
  • Both operations between the product and the sum are per-product and do not commute with addition. Per-term truncation does not distribute (sum of floor(p/2^12) is not floor(sum p /2^12)), the dropped bits 31:27 differ per term, and the zero-extension at bit 15 makes each negative term contribute an extra 2^15 modulo 2^16, so the tree's value depends on the count of negative non-zero terms, not just on the signed products. A chained ax*ay + chainin accumulation of raw products therefore does not reproduce the tree's output, and correcting it would need per-term soft logic (the low-12-bit remainder and a per-term 2^15 term) at least as large as the adders it replaced. Regrouping into the block's sums (the T2 option) is thus not available.
