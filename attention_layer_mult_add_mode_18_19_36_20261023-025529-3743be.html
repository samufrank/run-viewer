Every candidate sits either in the MVM datapath or in expunit's fixed-point multiply. The MVM stages are far shorter than the critical path: the longest of them is signedmul's result_ff (attention_layer.v:751) through the second half of vecmat_add's tree — four qadd2 levels (Add_u33->Add_u49->Add_u57->Add_u61, attention_layer.v:580-597) — about 0.060 + 0.96 + 4 x 1.382 = 6.55 ns, under 7.4498. 2. signedmul.$mul$attention_layer.v:741$74(sum_in=attention_layer)#1 (64 instances, the two 32-product trees of rv_acc_1/rv_acc_2) and #2 (64 instances, the 64-product tree of qk_acc) — left.

The multiply itself maps cleanly (result = a_ff*b_ff, attention_layer.v:741, with a_new/b_new the 16-bit magnitudes from attention_layer.v:739-740, both zero-extendable into ax[18]/ay[19], exact 32-bit product), and signedmul's two register stages (a_ff/b_ff at one edge, result_ff at the next, attention_layer.v:743-753) are exactly the block's operand-then-result pair, so a T1 conversion of the product alone would be behaviour-preserving (signedmul has no reset, so reset would be tied 0 and the registers would take their value from the first loads). It is left for two reasons:

  • Tiles rise, with nothing moving out of soft logic to pay for it. The 128 signedmul products occupy 97 tiles today (31 tiles with two products, 66 with one). A block of this mode takes a tile alone, so converting all 128 gives 128 + 4 (expunit) = 132 tiles, +31 x 253779 = +7.9e6 area (+9% on 8.74426e+07) with no compensating removal. Converting a subset is no better: the remaining soft multiplies repack at the packer's demonstrated density (1.32 products/tile), so for N converted the design lands near N + (128-N)/1.32 + 4 tiles, which is >= 101 for every N > 0; only a perfect 2-per-tile repack of the remainder at N = 66 would break even, and the baseline packing shows the packer does not reach that density. The only soft logic a conversion absorbs is result_ff's used bits (~16 BLEs per instance; the a_ff/b_ff bits pack for free with the a_new/b_new negate LUTs that drive them), i.e. at most ~2.9e6 area for a full conversion — less than the ~7.9e6 the extra tiles cost, and wirelength grows with the extra occupied tiles rather than shrinking.
  • The adder trees cannot follow the products into the blocks, so there is no second source of savings. bx and chainin are registered at the block's entry at the same edge as ax/ay and reach result two cycles later. The addends of these trees are the sibling products of the same vector: they only become valid at the edge at which the block's own result loads, two cycles too late to be applied at bx. Chaining through chainout->chainin does not rescue it either: chainout is only valid after the edge at which the upstream block registered its operands, so each further block in a chain must take its operands one edge later, i.e. the 64 operand pairs (all read from the same BRAM word on one cycle, attention_layer.v:132, 197, 202) would have to be skewed by up to 63 cycles of added flops, and the dot product's latency would grow from 4 cycles to 60+, which the unshifted control logic (out_wr_addr, wren_a, attention_layer.v:382-386) would expose on out_part1/out_part2 on the very first compared cycle. A bx-chained serial sum has the same latency problem (one register stage per product).

For completeness on the arithmetic, even a hypothetical regrouping would not be free: each term the tree adds is not the product but s_i * ((p_i >> 12) & 0x7FFF) (attention_layer.v:738), so the per-term 12-bit truncation and the 15-bit mask sit between the multiply and the add. The objective is area x delay x wirelength (delay register to register, 2026-10-20): VPR's total used logic block area (I/Os and empty grid tiles excluded) times the critical path times the total routed wirelength. Each term is the median over the loop's seeds; the product is the product of the medians. Beside every product stand the ratios against the baseline, A/A0 (area), D/D0 (critical path), W/W0 (wirelength) and their product, so the normalization can be read off. After each measured rewrite, the planner is given every measurement so far and asked for a revision. A revision is built and checked like the first rewrite, and the lowest product is kept, a later rewrite having to beat it by more than 8.58%. The loop stops when the planner declines to revise, when a revision does not lower the best product by more than 8.58%, when a revision fails its checks, or when 9 revisions have been made.
